# Geographical Indications — explanatory reference (DEMO)

This is a project-reference explanatory note, not the enacted statutory text.

A Geographical Indication (GI) protects products whose qualities, reputation,
or characteristics are essentially attributable to their geographic origin —
for example, a regional cultivar or a traditional regional preparation
method. Unlike a trademark, a GI is not owned by a single private company; it
is registered collectively, typically by an association of producers, and
individual producers from that region can apply to be recognised as
authorised users.

For Ayurveda businesses, GI protection is most relevant where a specific
region is closely tied to the reputation or characteristics of an ingredient
or preparation (for example, a variety grown or processed in a specific
locality using a locally rooted method), rather than for a company's own
proprietary formulation, which is more naturally protected via patent,
trademark, or trade-secret routes instead.
