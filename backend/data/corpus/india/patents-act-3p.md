# Section 3(p) — What is generally understood, in plain terms

This is a DEMO / project-reference explanatory note, not the enacted text of the
Patents Act, 1970. It is provided so the RAG pipeline has something concrete to
retrieve and cite while the project does not yet have the official statute PDF
ingested. Replace this file with the official Patents Act text (or an extract
of the relevant sections) for a production-accurate corpus.

In general terms, Indian patent law excludes from patentability an invention
which, in effect, amounts to the traditional knowledge of a community, or which
is an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known
components. This exclusion is commonly discussed under the heading "Section
3(p)" of the Patents Act, 1970 (as amended).

For an Ayurvedic formulation, this generally means:

- A formulation that simply restates or lightly aggregates ingredients and
  properties already documented in classical Ayurvedic texts is unlikely to be
  patentable on its own.
- A formulation, extraction process, delivery mechanism, or dosage form that
  goes meaningfully beyond documented traditional knowledge — demonstrating
  novelty and inventive step — may have a stronger patentability position,
  though this always requires a proper prior-art and legal assessment.
- Process patents (e.g. a novel extraction or stabilisation method) are
  sometimes viable even where a product patent on the underlying formulation
  is not.

This note does not reproduce statutory language and should not be treated as
a substitute for reading the Act itself or consulting a patent professional.
