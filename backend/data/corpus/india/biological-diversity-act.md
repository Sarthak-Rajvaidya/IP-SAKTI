# Biological Diversity Act — Access & Benefit Sharing (DEMO reference)

This is a project-reference explanatory note, not the enacted statutory text.

India regulates access to its biological resources and associated traditional
knowledge, and the sharing of benefits arising from their commercial use,
under the Biological Diversity Act, 2002 and related regulations. The
National Biodiversity Authority (NBA) at the central level, and State
Biodiversity Boards at the state level, are generally the relevant
authorities.

For an Ayurveda business, ABS considerations typically become relevant when:

- A formulation uses a biological resource (e.g. a specific herb, plant
  extract or its derivative) obtained from within India, and
- The intended use is commercial (manufacturing, selling, or otherwise
  monetising a product derived from that resource).

In such cases, prior approval or intimation to the relevant biodiversity
authority, and a benefit-sharing arrangement, may be required before
commercialisation. Purely non-commercial research use is sometimes subject to
a lighter compliance pathway, but this should be confirmed directly with the
relevant authority rather than assumed.
