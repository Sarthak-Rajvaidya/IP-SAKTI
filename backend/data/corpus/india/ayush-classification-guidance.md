# AYUSH product classification — explanatory reference (DEMO)

This is a project-reference explanatory note summarising commonly understood
regulatory categories, not an official Ministry of AYUSH publication.

Ayurvedic drug products in India are broadly understood to fall into
categories that commonly include:

1. Classical Ayurvedic medicines — formulations prepared strictly according
   to the formula and method described in the classical/authoritative texts
   recognised under the applicable drug rules, with no modification to the
   formula.
2. Proprietary Ayurvedic medicines — formulations that draw on Ayurvedic
   principles and ingredients but are not restricted to a single classical
   formula, and may include modifications, novel combinations, or new
   processing methods.
3. Ayurveda-Aahar / food and nutraceutical products — products positioned
   primarily as food/dietary supplements rather than medicines, which may
   instead fall under food-safety regulation.
4. Cosmetic products — products intended for cosmetic use, which follow a
   separate regulatory and labelling pathway.

The correct classification affects licensing requirements, permissible
claims, and — indirectly — the appropriate IP strategy (e.g. whether a
formulation is even a candidate for patent protection versus trademark-only
protection). Businesses should confirm classification with the relevant
State Licensing Authority or AYUSH guidance rather than self-certifying.
